## 0. The one-page plan

You do one thing well each week: pick a theme, talk it out into your app (3–5
minutes), and let the app refine it into a clean essay. That single essay becomes the
week's four posts — Technique, Myth-bust, Fear, Win — by changing the angle, not
writing four things. One of those posts also goes out to one external platform to
pull strangers toward the community. Every few weeks you gently show that the posts
themselves were made by your tool — which is the most honest app demo you could run.

That is the whole machine: one input → four community posts + one external post, with
the app as both the engine and the proof.

## 1. Positioning — the spine (broad front door, sharp hook)

Keep the broad personal-growth appeal, but lead with one recognizable idea so people
can repeat it. Your six weeks already share a single through-line: every one of them
is about lowering the internal friction between you and your best work — the friction
of switching, overload, freezing, letting go, and being understood.

**Proposed spine (one line):** Work with your wiring, not against it — do your best
work, and say it well, without grinding yourself down.

That single sentence is broad enough to cover focus, starting, mental load,
perfectionism, and communication — but specific enough that a member can tell a friend
"it's a community about doing good work without burning yourself out, and getting your
ideas across." That describability is what makes a free community grow.

Why this matters for the funnel: the "say it well" half of the spine is the natural
bridge to WillpowerLab. The app lives at the communication end of your own promise —
so pointing to it never feels like a bolt-on ad; it's the same message.

## 2. Content pillars (your "epics") — the source of infinite essays

Six recurring pillars, drawn straight from your draft. Each pillar is a well you

|---|---|---|---|

## 3. The Agile Content Engine (your content "product backlog")

You already run an agile system for the app; this is the same system for posts — same
vocabulary, so it feels familiar.

- **Pillar = Epic.** Focus, Mental load, and so on.
- **Week's theme = Story.** "This week: the Re-Entry Note."
- **The 4 posts + optional video = Tasks** under that story.
- **Backlog** = your bank of future weekly themes (Season 2, 3…).
- **The board:** a single Notion board or spreadsheet with columns — Ideas → Recorded
  → Drafted → Scheduled → Published.

### The rule that keeps you sane

A week only enters "Recorded" when you've done the one deep effort (the voice take).
Everything after that is derivation, not creation. You are never asked to write four
original things — the system forbids it.

### Batching & buffer

Aim to stay two weeks ahead. One focused 60–90 minute session produces a full week
(record the Technique via the app, then spin the other three from the templates in
section 4). Do it weekly, or batch two weeks once a fortnight — whichever fits your
energy. The buffer is what protects you on the weeks when even one essay is hard.

### Minimum Viable Week (the resilience valve)

On a bad week you are allowed to ship only: (a) the Win thread (members fill it — zero
writing) and (b) one recycled Myth-bust from the bank. The feed stays alive, nobody
notices, you don't break the streak. Growth dies from silence, not from an occasional
light week.

## 4. The repurposing recipe — one essay → four posts

This is the core mechanic. You produce one thing (the Technique essay, via the app).
The other three are the same idea rotated. Here is the exact move for each, so it's
mechanical:

**① TECHNIQUE (the anchor — your one real effort).** Record yourself explaining this
week's technique into the app: the science, the how, a try-it. Paste the refined text.
This is the only piece you "make." Keep your draft's shape — The science → The how →
Try it (you go first).

**② MYTH-BUST (derive by inversion).** Take the technique's core claim, name the false
belief it corrects, and flip it. Template: "Myth: [common belief]. → [the flip, in 3–4
lines]. [One-line reframe]." Costs ~2 minutes from the essay.

**③ FEAR (derive by confession).** Name the emotion underneath the technique — what the
member is quietly afraid of — confess yours first, then invite raised hands. Pure
vulnerability + a question. Drives the most comments of any format.

**④ WIN (derive by prompting — members write it, not you).** Turn the technique into a
share-your-example thread with an emoji + a warm prompt. Prime it with your example,
then react to every reply. Your engagement engine and lightest lift.

**Optional video (occasional booster, not a requirement).** A short talking-head of the
Myth-bust or Fear works well. Note your constraint: you can't export the app's
playback, so any video is recorded separately — treat it as a garnish you add when you
have energy, never a blocker. Text-first, always.

### Posting cadence across the week

| Day | Post | Why then |
|---|---|---|
| Mon | Technique | The anchor essay |
| Tue | Fear | Confession → comments early in the week |
| Thu | Myth-bust | Short, shareable, mid-week |
| Sat | Win | Member-fill thread → weekend engagement |

## 5. Growth engine (90 days, membership goal)

Honest mechanics first: Skool has weak built-in discovery. A free Skool community grows
from three forces, and with your one-essay capacity we lean hardest on the cheapest one.

**A. External mouth (top of funnel) — do ONE, well.** Your weekly Technique essay is
already written — repurpose it to one external platform where personal-growth content
is discoverable, ending with "the full version + the discussion is in [community], free
to join." With broad personal-growth plus a founder undertone, LinkedIn is the
highest-leverage low-production choice; if you'd rather be on camera, a short-form reel
works — but pick one and go deep. One external post/week is the entire paid-attention
engine.

**B. Internal engagement loops.** The Win threads are the loop: members post, you react
to every reply within 24h, and Skool rewards active communities with more surface.
Reacting to every early member personally is the single highest-ROI thing you can do in
month one — it doesn't scale forever, which is exactly why you do it now.

**C. Invite mechanics.** A monthly "who's one person this would help?" nudge, a
frictionless free join (no application wall while growing), and personal invites from
you to anyone who engages on your external posts — which convert far better than
passive discovery.

**D. The app-as-proof loop (your unfair advantage).** Roughly once every two weeks, show
the seam: "This post started as a 3-minute rambly voice note — my tool cleaned it into
this. That's the whole idea of WillpowerLab." Every post is quietly a product demo —
authentic, curiosity-feeding, and it makes the funnel feel like a reveal, not a pitch.

## 6. The funnel to WillpowerLab (free → app, without selling)

Free funnel = trust first, ask second. A soft CTA ladder, not a hard sell:

- **Passive (always on):** a pinned Welcome/About post that names the tool and offers
  early access / a waitlist link; one line in the community description — "built by the
  maker of WillpowerLab."
- **Contextual (weekly, gentle):** the Communicating-clearly pillar essays end with a
  soft mention — "I built a tool for exactly this; members get in first."
- **Demonstrative (biweekly):** the app-as-proof posts from section 5-D.
- **Event (monthly):** one "behind the scenes / try it with me" post or live, where you
  record something live and show the refined output.

Offer ladder: free community → app early access (free/beta, which also feeds your tester
+ annotation goals) → paid app usage later. The community's job in 90 days is volume of
trusting members at the top, so keep the app ask light and let the proof loop persuade.

## 7. Metrics — your weekly dashboard (5 numbers, no more)

Since the goal is membership growth, track leading indicators, not vanity:

1. New members this week (the goal metric).
2. Did the engine run? (Y/N — were the 4 posts published?) — protects the habit.
3. Comments per post (engagement → Skool reach).
4. External post → clicks → joins (is the top-of-funnel working?).
5. App early-access signups from the community (is the funnel converting?).

Review these once a week in ten minutes. If number 2 is ever "N," fix the system
(buffer, batching), not your motivation.

## 8. 90-day rollout

**Weeks 1–2 — Set up (light).** Build the board (Ideas → Recorded → Drafted → Scheduled
→ Published). Load your existing 24 posts as Season 1. Write the pinned Welcome + app
early-access post. Pick your one external channel. Choose posting days.

**Weeks 1–6 — Run Season 1 (already written — easy mode).** Publish the four formats
weekly from your draft. Your only new work is one external post/week (repurpose that
week's Technique) + reacting to every comment. Use these six weeks to build the habit
while the content cost is near zero.

**Weeks 4–6 — Turn on loops.** Start personal invites to anyone engaging externally. Add
the first app-as-proof post. Watch which external format pulls clicks.

**Weeks 7–12 — Season 2 via the engine.** Generate one new Technique per pillar, recorded
via the app, spun into four. Double down on whatever external format worked. Run your
first live/behind-the-scenes app demo. Re-evaluate the 5 metrics and decide whether to
introduce a paid tier later (you chose "free now" — revisit at day 90).

## 9. Open questions I didn't want to guess

None of these block you starting Season 1, but nail them before Season 2:

- **Community name & URL** — the draft says "willab"; is the community branded the same
  as the app (WillpowerLab) or distinct? Shared branding tightens the funnel.
- **External channel** — LinkedIn (recommended, low production) or short-form video?
  Pick one.
- **App CTA target** — is there a live waitlist / early-access link yet for the pinned
  post? If not, that's the one small thing to create first.
- **Your face on camera** — willing to do occasional talking-head, or strictly text?
  Decides whether video is ever in the mix.
- **Cadence reality-check** — is 4 posts/week right for a new small community, or start
  at 3 (Technique, Fear, Win) and add Myth-bust once there's an audience to share it?
  Fewer, consistent beats more, sporadic.
