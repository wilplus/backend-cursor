# Willonski — Marketing & Branding Strategy

A single working document covering positioning, brand, audience, and a 12‑month go‑to‑market plan for Willonski: an AI‑assisted, coach‑backed platform that trains people to speak with charisma and composure under stress.

This is a strategy document, not a marketing‑ops runbook. It is meant to make every downstream decision (copy, ads, landing pages, partnerships) faster and more consistent.

---

## 1. Product in one sentence

Willonski is a daily, 5‑minute speaking workout that scores how you actually sound — pace, fillers, energy, presence — and pairs that data with a human coach who watches your recordings and tells you what to fix next.

It is a hybrid product: AI does the measurement and the loop, a coach does the judgement and the relationship.

---

## 2. Where Willonski sits in the market

The "communication training" space splits into four camps:

| Camp | Examples | Promise | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Self‑help / books | TED talks, Vinh Giang on YouTube, "Talk Like TED" | Inspiration | No feedback loop, no accountability |
| Pure AI tools | Yoodli, Poised, Orai, Speeko | Instant metrics on your speech | Metrics without a human verdict feel like a fitness tracker without a trainer |
| Pure human coaching | Toastmasters, 1:1 executive coaches | Real feedback, real growth | Expensive, weekly cadence, no homework loop |
| Course platforms | Udemy, MasterClass speaking courses | Structured content | Passive; nothing measures the student |

Willonski's wedge is the middle: **AI for daily reps, human for verdicts.** That hybrid is what nothing else in the category does well, and it's what justifies a recurring price point above pure‑AI tools.

### Positioning statement

> For ambitious professionals who know their voice is holding them back, Willonski is the only daily speaking trainer that combines AI scoring with a real coach reviewing every session — so you actually change how you sound, not just learn about it.

---

## 3. Target audiences

Three concentric rings. Concentrate spend on Ring 1 until CAC and retention are proven, then expand.

### Ring 1 — Beachhead: ambitious individual contributors at tech / consulting / sales companies (25–40)

- They got told in a 360 or skip‑level: "you mumble," "you ramble," "you sound nervous in big meetings."
- They have disposable income (€50–€150/month is reasonable), are used to subscription tools, and self‑improve through apps (Duolingo, Headspace, Whoop).
- They will not pay €300/hr for a coach yet, but they want measurable progress.
- **Acquisition channels:** YouTube ads pre‑roll on charisma/public‑speaking content; LinkedIn thought‑leadership; Reddit (r/sales, r/cscareerquestions, r/PublicSpeaking); referral from frontline managers.

### Ring 2 — Career switchers and founders (28–45)

- Pitching investors, interviewing for senior roles, doing podcasts. High‑stakes spoken moments, infrequent, very expensive to mess up.
- Acquisition is content‑led: "I analysed 100 founder pitches — here's what kills them," "What VCs hear vs. what you said."

### Ring 3 — Non‑native English speakers in international workplaces

- The product already measures pace, fillers, and stress markers — these are exactly the dimensions ESL professionals struggle with.
- Latent demand is enormous (Europe, LATAM, India, SEA), and there's much less competition than the US "public speaking" niche.
- Acquisition: in‑language landing pages; partnerships with English schools and bootcamps; influencer partnerships in each market.

### Who Willonski is **not** for (yet)

- Enterprise L&D (long sales cycle, custom integrations) — Year 2.
- Children / K‑12 — different product entirely.
- "Professional speakers" — they already have coaches and stages.

Saying no to these audiences in the first 12 months is more valuable than saying yes to all of them.

## 4. Brand strategy

### 4.1 Brand promise

**You will sound different in 30 days. Measurably.**

Everything else (the AI, the coach, the daily reps, the chart) is in service of this single promise. The product already produces `performance_score_end` and a `performance_history` chart — the brand is the visible proof that this number moves.

### 4.2 Brand personality

Pick four traits, treat them as a forcing function for every creative decision.

| Trait | Means | Does not mean |
|---|---|---|
| **Direct** | "Your pace was 178 WPM. Slow down." | Harsh, condescending, drill‑sergeant |
| **Evidence‑led** | Every claim backed by your own data | Cold, robotic, jargon‑heavy |
| **Warm** | Your coach knows your name and your last three sessions | Hand‑holding, motivational fluff |
| **Quietly confident** | We don't need ALL CAPS or 🔥 to make the case | Boring, corporate, beige |

If a piece of copy fits all four, ship it. If it's missing **direct** or **warm**, it's a competitor's ad, not ours.

### 4.3 Voice and tone

- Short sentences. Verbs first.
- Numbers over adjectives. Never "much better" — always "+12 points in two weeks."
- Second person, present tense. "You filler 6 times per minute. Today we cut that in half."
- Coach‑voice, not influencer‑voice. We don't shout, we don't bro.
- One small unexpected delight per surface (a coach's handwritten‑style note, a chart that animates when you beat your prior best). The product is serious; the brand has a pulse.

### 4.4 Naming and verbal identity

- **Willonski** — short, weirdly memorable, ownable in search. Lean in. Do not corporate‑sanitise it.
- The product noun is **a workout** (not "a session," not "a lesson"). It frames the daily habit and aligns with the fitness‑for‑your‑voice metaphor.
- The output noun is **a verdict** (the coach's bit) and **a score** (the AI's bit). Keep them distinct in copy.
- Tagline candidates, in order of preference:
  1. **Train the way you sound.**
  2. Charisma is a metric now.
  3. Five minutes a day. A new voice in a month.

### 4.5 Visual identity (direction, not a system)

- **Palette:** dark navy / off‑black base, one warm signal colour (amber or coral) for "your score moved." Avoid the SaaS‑purple cliché and the EdTech‑green cliché both.
- **Typography:** a confident grotesque for headlines (Söhne, Inter Display, or similar), a humanist serif for coach‑voice quotes — the contrast makes the human/AI split legible at a glance.
- **Photography:** real users mid‑recording, headphones on, mouths actually moving. No stock "businessperson laughing at laptop." Imperfect lighting beats agency gloss here — it sells that this is the real, daily, slightly‑awkward act of practising.
- **Charts and data viz are the hero.** The progress chart from `performance_history` is the single most persuasive marketing asset we own. Build the brand around it.

## 5. The story to tell

Three messages, ranked by what we lead with depending on funnel stage.

1. **Top of funnel — "Your voice has a number."**
   The hook is the surprise that something you've never measured can be measured: pace, fillers, "charisma probability." Lead creative shows the chart climbing.

2. **Mid funnel — "AI watches every rep. A real coach watches every week."**
   Differentiates from Yoodli/Orai. The human‑in‑the‑loop is the moat; say so out loud.

3. **Bottom of funnel — "30 days, or your money back."**
   The product already tracks `performance_score_end` over time; the guarantee is the chart, not a vibe. This is cheap to offer because non‑improvers are also non‑practisers, and they self‑select out.

## 6. Go‑to‑market — 12 month plan

The plan is sequenced so each phase de‑risks the next. Don't skip ahead.

### Phase 0 (weeks 1–4) — Foundation
- Lock positioning, voice, and one tagline.
- Rebuild the marketing site around **a single hero asset: an animated `performance_history` chart with a real (anonymised) user's 30‑day journey.**
- Ship a free **"60‑second voice check"** — one recording, returns pace, fillers, and a charisma/stress estimate. No login. This is the top‑of‑funnel hook and the single most important asset to build first.
- Set up analytics: CAC by channel, activation (= first workout completed), week‑1 retention, day‑30 score delta.

### Phase 1 (months 2–3) — Beachhead acquisition
- **Content:** weekly teardown of a famous talk or pitch using Willonski's own metrics. This is content the product is uniquely qualified to produce — competitors cannot.
- **Paid:** small budget (€3–5k/mo) on YouTube pre‑roll against charisma / Vinh Giang / Chris Anderson / sales‑training audiences. LinkedIn against Sales Director / SDR / PM titles.
- **Founder‑led:** the founder posts one short Willonski‑analysed clip per workday on LinkedIn for 60 days. Cheap, compounding, on‑brand.
- **Target:** 500 paying users by end of month 3. CAC ceiling: €40 blended.

### Phase 2 (months 4–6) — Proof and referral
- Publish the first **Willonski Index** — aggregate (anonymised) stats: "the average tech worker speaks at 168 WPM and fillers 9× per minute." Press‑bait, link‑bait, repeatable annually.
- Ship in‑product **shareable progress card** ("I cut my fillers from 14 to 4 in 30 days") with a watermark. Make sharing one tap.
- Launch a referral mechanic (one month free per friend who completes 5 workouts). The product's score chart is the social proof — make sure the share asset shows it.
- **Target:** 2,000 paying users, 25% of new signups from referral, NPS ≥ 50.

### Phase 3 (months 7–9) — Expand audiences
- Open Ring 2 (founders) with a vertical landing page + a "pitch room" workout template.
- Pilot Ring 3 (ESL) with one language (Spanish or Portuguese) — full localised funnel, one in‑market coach on staff.
- First paid partnerships: 3–5 mid‑tier creators in business / sales / public‑speaking niches, performance deal (not flat fee).
- **Target:** 5,000 paying users; Ring 2 at ≥15% of new signups; first Spanish‑speaking cohort live.

### Phase 4 (months 10–12) — Defensibility and stretch
- Begin a closed pilot with **3 enterprise design partners** (sales orgs, consultancies). Goal is not revenue yet — it's a logo wall and product feedback for Year 2.
- Public **30‑day money‑back guarantee** based on the score chart. By now we have enough data to know this is safe.
- Strategic content investment: a longform research piece ("What makes founders sound fundable") backed by Willonski data, pitched to one tier‑1 publication.
- **Target:** 10,000 paying users; <€35 blended CAC; ≥40% month‑6 retention.

## 7. Pricing and packaging (brand‑level, not finance‑level)

Three tiers. The middle one is where the brand lives.

- **Free — Daily Score.** One workout per day, AI score only, no coach. Permanent free tier; it's both the funnel and the product's most credible marketing.
- **Coached — €29/mo (or €19/mo annual).** Daily workouts, full AI scoring, **a real coach reviews 1 workout per week** with a written verdict. This is the default product and the one all marketing should sell.
- **Pro — €79/mo.** Daily workouts, coach reviews 3 per week, async chat with the coach, custom focus tasks (the product already supports `selected_task_id` and coaching memory — this is where to surface it).

Pricing principles:

- Anchor on "what 1 hour of a real coach would cost" (€150–€300), not on "what an AI app costs." Every paid tier should feel like getting a coach for the price of Spotify.
- Never discount the **Coached** tier on the landing page. Discounts go to the free tier in the form of a longer trial.
- Annual price ends in 9, monthly does not. Small thing, real conversion impact.

## 8. Channels — what to invest in, what to ignore

**Invest:**
- YouTube pre‑roll on adjacent creator content. Highest‑intent video traffic on the open web for this audience.
- LinkedIn organic, founder‑led. The audience is here, paid is expensive, organic is undervalued.
- SEO on long‑tail diagnostic queries: "why do I say um so much," "how to slow down when speaking," "how to sound more confident on Zoom." The free voice check is the conversion event for all of these.
- Lifecycle email. The product has rich per‑user state (`recurring_issues`, `last_5_scores`); the emails should be the most personalised in the category — "Your fillers dropped 30% this week" beats every newsletter.

**Ignore (for now):**
- Google search ads against high‑CPC head terms ("public speaking course"). We will lose to Udemy / MasterClass on budget.
- TikTok. Wrong audience density for Ring 1, expensive to learn, easy to defer to Year 2.
- Podcast ads at scale. Too noisy for early CAC reads; revisit once we have a podcast partner of our own.
- Cold outbound. Wrong motion for a €29/mo product.

## 9. Metrics that matter (and what they signal)

| Metric | What it tells the brand | Target by month 12 |
|---|---|---|
| **CAC (blended)** | Whether the story is working in market | <€35 |
| **Activation = first workout completed** | Whether the onboarding is converting curiosity into reps | ≥70% of signups |
| **Day‑7 retention** | Whether daily workouts actually feel daily | ≥40% |
| **Day‑30 score delta (median)** | Whether the brand promise is being kept | ≥ +10 points |
| **Coach‑verdict open rate** | Whether the human side of the product is loved | ≥80% |
| **Referral share rate** | Whether the score chart is as shareable as we think | ≥10% of completers share |

The single most important one is **day‑30 score delta.** If the chart moves, every other number eventually moves with it. If it doesn't, no amount of marketing fixes the funnel.

## 10. Risks and how the brand answers them

- **"It's just another AI app."** Answer: lead every surface with the coach. The coach's verdict is the receipt that this isn't ChatGPT‑for‑voice.
- **"My data sounds private — is it safe?"** Answer: ship a clear, public stance on what we store, why, and for how long. Recordings are sensitive; treat them with the seriousness Apple treats Health data, not the casualness most EdTech treats homework.
- **"Coaches don't scale."** True. The product already separates the AI loop from the coach loop — design coach workflows so 1 coach can serve 200+ Coached‑tier users (queue, templates, prefilled drafts from the LLM the team is already fine‑tuning). The brand has to keep coaches central even while ops gets ruthlessly efficient.
- **"What if the score is wrong?"** The L1/L2 learning loops already in the architecture address this on the product side. On the brand side: be public about how the score is computed, version it, and never claim certainty we don't have. Calibrated honesty here is a moat.

## 11. First 30 days — a concrete checklist

A strategy that doesn't translate into next‑Monday actions isn't a strategy. In priority order:

1. **Ship the free 60‑second voice check.** No login. Returns three numbers and one sentence. This is the single most important marketing asset in the next 12 months.
2. **Rewrite the homepage hero around one animated chart and the line "Train the way you sound."**
3. **Pick one coach to be the public face for the next 90 days.** Real name, real photo, real verdicts shown (with permission). The brand needs a human.
4. **Set up the analytics stack** (CAC by channel, activation, D7/D30, score delta). Without this, every later decision is vibes.
5. **Founder posts one Willonski‑analysed clip per workday for 30 days on LinkedIn.** Costs nothing, generates the first proof points.
6. **Draft and publish the data + privacy stance** as a real page, not a footer link. It will get cited and shared more than expected.

If those six things are done well, everything in §6 becomes executable. If they aren't, no amount of paid spend matters.
