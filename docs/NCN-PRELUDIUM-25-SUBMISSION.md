## SECTION 5 — STRESZCZENIE (EN, max 4500 chars including spaces)

A growing literature warns of metacognitive degradation from AI in learning environments. Fan et al. (2025) document *metacognitive laziness*; Gerlich (2025) describes *cognitive offloading* and the erosion of critical thinking. These findings are reshaping educational policy and organisational decisions about AI in Learning & Development. Yet all such studies have examined a single deployment pattern: AI deployed as a **substitute** for cognition.

This project tests a competing hypothesis: when AI is architected as a **metacognitive scaffold** — surfacing the learner's own behaviour to provoke self-directed reflection rather than supplying answers — the predicted negative effects should reverse. A null or negative result corroborates Fan and Gerlich. A positive result identifies the boundary condition their substitutive-AI studies were structurally unable to detect.

The test takes place in a strategically important context: development of communication competence in technology professionals. This population consistently resists conventional 1:1 coaching because exposing incompetence in front of an evaluator triggers *evaluation apprehension* (Cottrell, 1972) and blocks the very metacognition coaching aims to develop. An asynchronous architecture removes the evaluator; an AI layer surfaces precise acoustic data about the learner's voice from which they discover patterns themselves.

**Stage I (Months 7–24)** is a three-arm randomised controlled trial (N ≈ 60) comparing: (A) conventional synchronous video coaching; (B) asynchronous human coaching without AI; (C) asynchronous human coaching plus AI-driven acoustic biofeedback. Arm B is methodologically essential — it isolates the effect of asynchrony from AI augmentation, a confound that has weakened prior research. Outcome assessment is multi-method convergent: cognitive, skill-based and affective learning outcomes (Kraiger, Ford & Salas, 1993); 90-day transfer (Blume et al., 2010); and independent neurocognitive validation via EEG signatures of Flow (frontal alpha asymmetry, frontal midline theta; Katahira et al., 2018) at the Interdepartmental Laboratory of Neuropsychophysiology.

**Stage II (Months 19–33)** is a 12-week field study (N ≥ 100) validating macro-acoustic telemetry as a scalable HR-analytics instrument under ecological conditions. Three validation paths: (i) on-device acoustic logs against self-reported Flow trajectories; (ii) Intra-Speaker Baseline models against population-average baselines; (iii) acoustic markers against peer-rated charismatic delivery.

The measurement instrument is **willab**, a Progressive Web Application built by the candidate with a privacy-by-design architecture (on-device feature extraction; only aggregated, de-identified features transmitted). willab is not the object of evaluation — it is the validated instrument that makes the research questions tractable.

Three contributions are anticipated. **Theoretical:** a falsifiable test of AI-as-scaffold vs. AI-as-substitute, refining feedback-intervention theory (Kluger & DeNisi, 1996). **Methodological:** the first independent EEG validation of Flow-onset acceleration in organisational coaching, plus validation of consumer-grade acoustic telemetry. **Practical:** an evidence-based deployment model for AI in workplace learning that avoids the metacognitive degradation documented by Fan and Gerlich.

## SECTION 14 — OPIS SKRÓCONY (EN, max 5 pages A4 + bibliography)

### 1. Scientific Goal

This project tests whether asynchronous AI-augmented coaching — architected as a metacognitive scaffold rather than as a cognitive substitute — produces measurably superior learning outcomes than conventional synchronous 1:1 coaching among technology professionals, and identifies the neurocognitive and acoustic mechanisms accounting for any observed difference.

**Primary research question.** Does asynchronous coaching augmented by AI-driven acoustic biofeedback produce greater learning outcomes (Kraiger, Ford & Salas, 1993) — across cognitive, skill-based, and affective dimensions — than synchronous 1:1 coaching, and is this effect mediated by reductions in evaluation apprehension and accelerated Flow onset?

**Secondary questions.**
- *RQ2 (Mechanism):* Do EEG signatures of Flow (frontal alpha asymmetry, frontal midline theta, reduced DLPFC engagement; Katahira et al. 2018; Dietrich 2004) emerge faster and more reliably under AI augmentation, and do they correlate with self-reported Flow on validated scales?
- *RQ3 (Methodological — Valence Ambiguity):* Can macro-acoustic markers (F0 variance, speech-to-pause ratio, disfluency rate, WPM stability) reliably differentiate threat-arousal from challenge-arousal states (Blascovich & Mendes 2010), providing a scalable alternative to laboratory biometrics?
- *RQ4 (Methodological — Idiosyncrasy Limit):* Does the asynchronous architecture support construction of individual Intra-Speaker Baselines that outperform population-average models in detecting within-person communication-competence change over time?

**Hypotheses (pre-registered).**
- H1: Post-willab > pre-willab on composite learning outcomes (within-subject, paired comparison; Cohen's *d_z* ≥ 0.5 expected at Event 2 vs Event 1).
- H2: willab arm > waitlist arm on improvement magnitude (between-subjects, isolates intervention effect from mere conference-practice / maturation effects).
- H3: Post-willab > pre-willab on Flow EEG signatures (faster time-to-emergence of frontal alpha asymmetry + midline theta during Event 2 talk vs Event 1 talk in willab arm; smaller or no shift in waitlist arm).
- H4: Reduction in evaluation apprehension mediates willab-arm advantage.
- H5: Macro-acoustic markers discriminate threat- vs. challenge-arousal with sensitivity ≥ 0.75, specificity ≥ 0.70 against EEG ground truth.
- H6: Intra-Speaker Baselines outperform population baselines for within-person change detection (AUC improvement ≥ 0.10).

### 2. Significance — State of the Art

**Three converging problems define the gap.**

*First, the training-transfer problem.* Meta-analytic evidence (Baldwin & Ford 1988; Blume et al. 2010) shows organisations recover only a small fraction of training spend in observable workplace behaviour change. For soft skills among technical experts the gap is more pronounced. Synchronous coaching is meta-analytically effective (Theeboom et al. 2014; Jones et al. 2016) but its effect size is conditional on the working alliance (Grant 2014; de Haan et al. 2016) — precisely what high-status, high-evaluation-anxiety populations resist.

*Second, the AI paradox in workplace learning.* Recent literature converges on a worrying finding: AI use degrades critical thinking and metacognitive engagement (Fan et al. 2025; Gerlich 2025). These results are reshaping educational policy and organisational decisions. However, all such studies examined AI deployed as a **substitute** for cognition. None tested AI deployed as a **scaffold** surfacing the learner's own behaviour. This project positions itself directly against the emerging consensus: a three-arm design with EEG validation provides a falsifiable test. A null on Arm C corroborates Fan and Gerlich; a positive identifies the boundary condition their substitutive-AI studies could not detect.

*Third, three methodological obstacles in communication-competence assessment.* (a) *Valence Ambiguity* — physiological arousal is indistinguishable between threat and challenge states using standard biometrics (Blascovich & Mendes 2010). (b) *Dirty Audio Wall* — reliance on studio-grade acoustic instrumentation that does not scale to real organisational contexts. (c) *Idiosyncrasy Limit* — population-average models fail to capture individual baselines, where within-person change actually occurs. The willab platform was built precisely to address all three.

**Originality.** No prior study has (i) simultaneously isolated asynchrony from AI augmentation through a dedicated experimental arm, (ii) provided independent EEG validation of Flow-onset acceleration in workplace coaching, or (iii) validated consumer-grade acoustic telemetry against laboratory measures for HR analytics. The interdisciplinary integration of management science with cognitive neuroscience — institutionalised through the Interdepartmental Laboratory of Neuropsychophysiology collaboration — is itself a contribution.

### 3. Concept and Research Plan

**Two-stage sequential design.**

*Stage I (M7–M24):* Three-arm RCT with EEG validation. N ≈ 66 (60 + buffer). *A priori* power analysis (G*Power 3.1, one-way ANOVA, α = .05, 1−β = .80, *f* = 0.40) confirms N = 66 sufficient for medium effect detection. Stratified randomisation (sex, years of experience) into three arms of N = 20 + 2 buffer. Six-week intervention per cohort, three rolling cohorts. Pre/post-intervention measurements at three time points; 90-day transfer follow-up.

*Stage II (M19–M33):* 12-week field validation. N ≥ 100 (independent sample). Pure ecological validation — no coach involvement. Three cross-validation paths: (i) on-device acoustic logs vs. weekly self-reported Flow; (ii) Intra-Speaker Baseline vs. population-average models; (iii) acoustic markers vs. peer-rated charismatic delivery of a standardised end-of-period presentation.

### 4. Methodology

**Stage I dependent variables — multi-method convergent assessment:**
- *Cognitive outcomes:* Declarative + strategic knowledge of charismatic leadership tactics (Antonakis et al. 2011), pre/post.
- *Skill-based outcomes:* Expert-rated CLT acquisition from blinded video samples of a standardised stakeholder-presentation task, scored by two raters (Cohen's κ target ≥ 0.75).
- *Affective outcomes:* Training self-efficacy (Bandura 1997, adapted); evaluation apprehension scale; Flow Short Scale (Rheinberg, Vollmeyer & Engeser 2003) after each practice session.
- *Transfer outcomes (90-day):* Supervisor ratings + self-reports of workplace application (Blume et al. 2010 framework).
- *Neurocognitive validation (EEG):* Frontal alpha asymmetry (F3/F4), frontal midline theta (Fz), time-to-emergence of Flow-correlated signatures during in-lab practice sessions. 32-channel system, standard 10-20 montage, 1000 Hz sampling, online band-pass 0.1–100 Hz. Conducted at the Interdepartmental Laboratory of Neuropsychophysiology.

**Statistical analysis.**
- Primary: mixed-effects ANCOVA on composite learning outcome (pre-test as covariate, arm as fixed effect, participant as random intercept).
- Mediation: bootstrap mediation analysis (PROCESS macro; 10,000 resamples) for H4.
- EEG time-to-criterion: Cox regression for H3.
- Multiple comparisons: Holm-Bonferroni across six primary hypotheses.

**Stage II analysis.** Hierarchical linear modelling for longitudinal Flow trajectories; ROC analysis for baseline-model comparison (H6); inter-rater reliability via ICC(2,k) for peer-rating ground truth.

**Instrument.** willab — on-device acoustic feature extraction (pace, pause structure, dynamic range, pitch center via autocorrelation with confidence ≥ 0.15, pitch variance, emphasis-per-minute, energy ratio, filler-word density). Per-participant rolling baseline from first 5 recordings; subsequent recordings expressed as z-scores against the participant's own baseline. Privacy-by-design: raw audio never transmitted.
