## 0. Why this DPIA exists, and the fact that it is late

Art 35(1) requires a DPIA **prior to** processing. Recording and inference have
been running in production since before this date. **This DPIA is therefore
late, and that is itself a compliance finding** — recorded here rather than
hidden, because a regulator will establish the date from the repository history
in minutes and the candour is worth more than the concealment.

Mitigating facts to be confirmed (see §8 OPEN-1): the founder's position as of
2026-09-17 is that the service has **no active user base beyond the operator and
a small number of test/pilot accounts**. If confirmed, the number of data
subjects affected by the delay is very small, which bears directly on Art 83(2)(a)
(nature, gravity and duration) and 83(2)(k). **This must be verified against
`user_consents` before the DPIA is adopted — the whole risk assessment scales
off that number.**

## 1. Art 35(3) — why a DPIA is mandatory here

Not discretionary. The processing meets several independent triggers:

| Trigger | Source | Met? |
|---|---|---|
| Systematic and extensive evaluation of personal aspects by automated processing | Art 35(3)(a) | **Yes** — delivery profiling across repeated takes, per speaker, normalised to that speaker's own baseline |
| Processing of special-category data on a large scale | Art 35(3)(b) | Contested — see §4. Controller adopts a cautious posture |
| Evaluation or scoring | WP248 rev.01 criterion 1 | **Yes** |
| Systematic monitoring | WP248 criterion 3 | **Yes** — every take recorded and analysed |
| Innovative use of new technology | WP248 criterion 7 | **Yes** — acoustic state inference |
| Data concerning vulnerable subjects (employee/employer imbalance) | WP248 criterion 6 | **Conditional** — see §5, B2B exposure |

WP248 recommends a DPIA where **two** criteria are met. Four are met
unconditionally. There is no reasonable argument that a DPIA is not required.

## 2. Art 35(7)(a) — Systematic description of the processing

### 2.1 Nature and scope

A user records spoken "takes" against slides in their browser. Audio is uploaded
to object storage, transcribed, segmented per slide, and analysed. The system
generates a canonical "Ideal Text" and evidence-backed coaching feedback. A
human coach may review recordings asynchronously. Users may opt in to share
extracts with other users for blind peer rating.

### 2.2 Categories of data subject

1. **Users** (speakers) — the primary subjects.
2. **Coaches** — their labels and corrections are personal data about them.
3. **Peer raters** — their perceptual judgements are personal data about them.
4. **Incidental third parties** — any person whose voice or personal data appears
   in a user's recorded content. Terms §8 places the obligation on the user to
   obtain consent. **This transfers responsibility but does not eliminate
   controller risk** — see §6 RISK-7.

### 2.3 Categories of personal data

## 4. The Article 9 question

### 4.1 The controller's published position
Privacy §3: voice is processed to analyse delivery, not to uniquely identify;
no voiceprints; therefore Art 9 is not triggered by the processing itself, but a
cautious Art 9(2)(a) posture is adopted in case.

### 4.2 Assessment
**The core reasoning is correct.** Art 4(14) defines biometric data by reference
to processing "for the purpose of uniquely identifying a natural person."
WillpowerLab does not identify; it measures delivery. Recital 51 supports the
purpose-limb reading. Voice recordings are not *per se* Art 9 data.

Two residual routes into Art 9 remain open:
1. **Content.** A user may disclose health, religious, political or sexual-life
   information in a practice presentation. This is unsolicited and incidental,
   but it is foreseeable. The cautious 9(2)(a) posture covers it.
2. **Inference.** If a delivery signal were ever treated as indicating a
   psychological or health state, that inference would be Art 9 data about
   mental health. The construct fence and AC-9 are the controls that keep this
   closed. **They must hold.**

### 4.3 ⚠️ The AI Act does not follow this reasoning — the critical carry-over error
**AI Act Art 3(34) defines "biometric data" WITHOUT the unique-identification
limb** that GDPR Art 4(14) requires. Pitch, loudness dynamics, speech rate and
pause structure are physiological and behavioural characteristics derived by
specific technical processing. **Under the AI Act this is biometric data, even
though under GDPR it is not.**

Any conclusion of the form "we do not do voice ID, therefore we are outside the
biometric regime" is **valid for GDPR and invalid for the AI Act.** This is the
single most consequential legal point in this assessment and is the subject of
the counsel memo at `docs/legal/AI-ACT-SCOPING-MEMO.md`.

## 5. AI Act interaction — assessed here because it drives the GDPR risk

`services/voice_confidence.py` infers a position on a **confidence↔doubt
spectrum** from acoustic features, z-scored **against the individual speaker's
own baseline**. On the AI Act's broader biometric definition this is capable of
meeting Art 3(39) "emotion recognition system."

**Assessment: more likely than not in scope. Not free from doubt.** Full
argument in the counsel memo. Two consequences:

- **Art 5(1)(f) — prohibited in workplace and education.** Applicable since
  2 Feb 2025. **The founder has confirmed that B2B interest already exists.**
  This moves the risk from theoretical to active. Terms §7 bans employer and
  institutional use, which is real mitigation on the "intended purpose" limb,
  but it is **contractual only — nothing in the code prevents a company buying
  seats or a coach assigning takes to employees.**
- **Annex III(1)(c) — high-risk otherwise.** The Art 6(3) "no significant risk"
  derogation is unavailable because its final subparagraph makes any Annex III
  system always high-risk where it performs **profiling**, which this does.

**Date caveat:** the Annex III application date (2 Aug 2026) may have been
altered by the Commission's Digital Omnibus proposal. **Counsel must confirm the
position as at the date of reading.** This DPIA does not assume either answer.

**Art 4(11), 7(4), Recital 43. Severity: HIGH. Likelihood: HIGH.**

`legal/mlc2-bundled-consent-v1.json` sets `bundled_ui: true`,

- M1.4 Retain Art 9(2)(a) explicit consent as the cautious overlay for incidental special-category content.
- M1.5 Ship the exclusion flag so an objection can be honoured within Art 12(3)'s month (closes backlog L-1).

### 🔴 RISK-2 — Published copy contradicts the code
**Art 5(1)(a) fairness and transparency; Art 13. Severity: MEDIUM-HIGH. Likelihood: HIGH.**

Privacy §6 and Terms §7 both state the voice inference "is opt-in and off by
default." `services/voice_confidence.py:256`, in `enabled()`, defaults
`VOICE_CONFIDENCE_ENABLED` to `"1"` — on — and it is an environment variable, not
a per-user consent flag. No per-user opt-in for the §6 inference exists. What
defaults off is `VOICE_CONFIDENCE_RANKING_ENABLED` (`:248`, in
`ranking_enabled()`), a different question.

It also contradicts §3 and §5 of the same document, which state the consent
covering the inference is **required** to use the service. The inference cannot
simultaneously be mandatory and opt-in.

**Measures:**
- M2.1 Amend both documents to describe what the system does. (Drafted; awaiting founder sign-off under the LIVE LOOP fence.)
- M2.2 *Or* build a genuine per-user flag and gate computation on it. Preferred if the inference is to remain consent-based.
- M2.3 Confirm the production value of `VOICE_CONFIDENCE_ENABLED` on every Railway service **from the boot log, not the dashboard** (CONFIG-FIRST rule, `docs/MIGRATIONS.md`).

### 🔴 RISK-3 — AI Act emotion-recognition exposure, with live B2B interest
**AI Act Art 5(1)(f) / Annex III(1)(c). Severity: SEVERE. Likelihood: MEDIUM.**

Ceiling of €35m or 7% of worldwide turnover for an Art 5 breach. For an
unregistered sole trader the practical ceiling is lower, but a prohibition
finding is existential to the product regardless of the fine.

**Measures:**
- M3.1 Counsel opinion on Art 3(39) scope. (Memo drafted.)
- M3.2 **Technical** enforcement of the B2C fence: no team plans, no seat purchasing, no employer dashboards, no organisational billing. Contract terms alone do not discharge Art 5.
- M3.3 If any company has already used the service, establish dates and scope — this determines whether there is a historical breach to remediate. **OPEN-2.**
- M3.4 Consider removing the **speaker-relative baseline**, the design feature that most strongly characterises the output as a claim about a person rather than about audio. Accepted cost: measurable loss of F1 accuracy (the source literature normalises within-speaker).
- M3.5 Keep the blind shadow model (`mlc2_confidence_blind.py`) strictly off-product. A learned classifier of human confidence judgements is the least defensible artefact in the system.
- M3.6 Preserve and extend the existing AI notice receipt path (`routes/v2/processing_authorization.py`) — it already discharges much of Art 50(3).

### 🟠 RISK-4 — Data-subject rights cannot be served

### 🟢 RISK-5 — Storage limitation: audio retention
**Art 5(1)(e). Severity: LOW. Likelihood: LOW.**
**Downgraded 2026-09-17 from 🟠 MEDIUM/HIGH.**

This risk was raised against Privacy §10's Voice Data criterion, which resolved
to "as long as consent is active" — indefinite in practice, with no purge job in
any Railway cron.

**Superseded by an approved schedule.** Two retention positions were recorded on
2026-09-17. The earlier one — life of account, purged on closure — was recorded
here against this assessment's advice of a fixed 90-day maximum. The founder has
since confirmed that the authoritative position is the schedule in
`legal/phase1-2026.1/06-retention-schedule-v1.0`:

| Category | Period |
|---|---|
| Audio recordings | **12 months after last use** |
| Voice measurements | deleted with their source audio, never after it |
| Transcripts, Ideal Text, feedback | until account deletion |
| Practice attempts | 30 days after the practice closes |
| Unreferenced uploads | 24 hours |
| Security and technical logs | 90 days |

**Why this downgrades rather than merely restates.** The original finding was not
that the period was too long; it was that there was **no period at all** — only
an intention conditional on a deletion route that did not exist. A fixed
12-month criterion running from last use is a storage-limitation answer that
holds whether or not the user ever closes their account, which is what
Art 5(1)(e) asks for. Voice measurements are additionally bound to their source,
so the most sensitive derived artifact cannot outlive the recording it came from
— the position that was hardest to defend under Art 5(1)(e) and, had the US
proceeded, under BIPA §15(a).

**Residual risk: LOW, and no longer accepted-as-is.** The schedule is published
at `willpowerlab.com/legal/retention` and is the document Art 13(2)(a) requires.

**What is left is execution, not policy.** The retention rules are seeded by a
pending migration (`data_retention_rules`, five categories, held out of
`manifest.txt` until document 06 is signed), and the periods still need the job
that enforces them.

**Measures:**
- M5.1 Build the purge job to the schedule above. The dependency on M4.1 stands:
  until deletion works, a written period is still only an intention. **L-4 and
  L-5 remain one deliverable.**
- M5.2 Publish the schedule and reference it from Privacy §7 — drafted, pending
  founder sign-off on the v2.0 copy.
- M5.3 The voice-measurement rule is a field-level JSONB redaction rather than a
  row delete, because the stamp shares a row with transcript text that outlives
  it. Confirm the approach before building (document 06 §3).
- M5.4 Revisit at launch and at any material growth in user numbers.

**Counsel question withdrawn.** This assessment previously asked counsel whether
indefinite retention was defensible. It no longer needs to; the question was
made moot by the schedule rather than answered.

### 🔴 RISK-10 — User content disclosed to a third party for model training, contrary to the published notice
**Art 5(1)(a), 6, 13, 28, 33, 34. Severity: FOR FOUNDER AND COUNSEL TO SET.
Likelihood: MATERIALISED — this is not a risk of something happening.**

Recorded as its own risk rather than folded into RISK-2 (published copy
contradicts the code) or RISK-8 (sub-processor assertions unverified). Those two
describe statements that were unverified. This describes processing that
occurred: a disclosure to a processor for a purpose the privacy notice expressly
denied, which is a different act with different obligations attached.

**What happened.** On 2026-09-17 all three data-sharing settings on the OpenAI
organisation were found enabled, including *"Share inputs and outputs with
OpenAI… including for improving and training our models"*, accepted in exchange
for complimentary daily API tokens. Throughout, the published Privacy Policy

general-purpose or foundation models.

**Scope, as far as it can currently be established.** 1,708 audio recordings
across 25 distinct users; oldest 8 May 2026; most recent 17 September 2026, the
day of discovery; 590 recordings across 4 users carry a delivery-signal value.

**Why the affected window cannot be bounded.** The date the settings were
enabled is unknown and is not estimated anywhere in this document. Audit logging
on the OpenAI organisation was not enabled at the time, so no local record
exists. OpenAI has been asked whether their own records can supply it. Until
they answer, the window is *up to* the four months above and cannot be narrowed
from our side. It may prove unrecoverable.

**Measures:**
- M10.1 All three sharing settings disabled. **DONE 2026-09-17.** Forward-only —
  anything already shared cannot be recalled.
- M10.2 Audit logging enabled on the OpenAI organisation, which it was not at
  the time. **DONE 2026-09-17.** This does not reconstruct the past; it means the
  next such question is answerable.

### 🔴 RISK-11 — User recordings are served from a public bucket over permanent unauthenticated URLs
**Art 5(1)(f), 32. Severity: FOR FOUNDER AND COUNSEL TO SET. Likelihood:
MATERIALISED — this is the current serving architecture, not a risk of
something happening.**

Found 2026-09-17 while configuring the MLC-3 practice bucket, from the
Cloudflare console rather than from the code. Recorded separately from RISK-6
(coach review is a real disclosure), which concerns *authorised* recipients.
This is about the absence of any access control at all.

#### The finding

`coach-feedback-videos` (382 objects, 2.24 GB) has **Public Access: Enabled**
and is served through a Cloudflare **public development URL**,
`R2_PUBLIC_BASE_URL = https://pub-62a94c47b9a741d8b5b4bea704f13447.r2.dev`.
Cloudflare's own console labels that URL form rate-limited and *not recommended
for production*. `user-interview-audio` (110 objects) is served the same way via
`R2_AUDIO_PUBLIC_BASE_URL`.

Personal data under those public prefixes:

| Prefix | Contents | Written by |
|---|---|---|
| `session_recordings/<sid>/full.webm` | concatenated full session audio | `services/session_concatenation.py` |
| `guest_funnel/<sid>/turn_N.webm` | per-turn guest audio | pre-account funnel |
| `willab_lab/<sid>/recording_<hex>` | uploaded Take audio | `services/lab_recording_persistence.py:71` |
| `charisma_snippets/<uuid>` | extracted clips of user speech | `services/snippet_extraction.py:126` |
| `willab_presentations/<uuid>.pdf` | uploaded slide decks | `routes/v2/lab_recording.py:147` |

**The code prefers the unauthenticated form.** At five call sites the pattern is
`coach_media_public_url(key) or presigned_get_coach_object(key, ...)`
(`routes/v2/lab_recording.py:153`, `routes/v2/user_account.py:664`,
`services/coach_video_storage.py:134` and `:196`,
`services/tutor_video_url.py:203`). Because `R2_PUBLIC_BASE_URL` is set on both
Railway services, the signed branch is effectively dead. `coach_video_storage.py`
additionally **rewrites stored signed URLs into public ones on read** — a
deliberate 2026-09-16 repair for decks that expired after seven days, which has
the side effect of moving the estate toward permanent public addressing.

#### What the exposure actually is

Object keys embed UUIDs, so the namespace is not enumerable and there is no
directory listing. The exposure is therefore not "anyone can browse the
recordings"; it is that **any single URL, once it leaves the system, grants
permanent unauthenticated access to that recording, cannot be revoked, and
cannot be expired** — short of deleting the object. URLs leave systems routinely:
forwarded messages, browser history, referrer headers, screenshots, support
tickets, logs.

This is an Art 32 "appropriate technical measures" question. Signed, short-lived
URLs are the ordinary control for exactly this, the codebase already implements
them (`presigned_get_coach_object`), and they were the original design — the
public path was introduced to fix an expiry bug, not as a security decision.

**No transfer issue.** All four R2 buckets are located in **Eastern Europe
(EEUR)**, verified in the Cloudflare console 2026-09-17. This risk is about
access control, not about where the data sits. §2.5 is unaffected.

#### Why it is being remediated now rather than scheduled

Under the §0 assumption of a very small user base this would have been a
scheduled fix. That assumption failed (see the §6 banner), and the founder
advises a **significant traffic increase within days**. The control therefore has
to be in place before the population it protects arrives, not after.

> **Implementation status, 2026-09-18 — read the citations above as the state on
> 2026-09-17, the assessment date.** M11.1-M11.3 have since landed and the
> assessment text above has deliberately NOT been rewritten around them: the risk
> rating, the residual at M11.4 and the counsel question at M11.6 are the
> controller's record as assessed, and re-dating them would destroy the evidence
> of what was found. What changed in code:
>
> - `services/user_content_keys.py` is the single list of user-content prefixes,

>   `or presigned_...` fallback at the five call sites above is the live branch

> - `refreshed_media_url` mints a fresh signature from the object key on every
>   read rather than trusting a stored URL (`services/coach_video_storage.py:126`,

> - **Decks did not keep the public path.** M11.1 as drafted excluded
>   `willab_presentations/`; `willab_presentations/` is in fact on the

> - **M11.4 is unchanged and still open.** Both buckets remain publicly readable

**Measures:**
- **M11.1** Stop minting public URLs for user-content prefixes; fall through to
  short-lived signed URLs at every call site. Deck and coach-authored media

- **M11.5** Record in the Art 30 register that user content was served over

### 🟠 RISK-12 — Erasure is non-functional, and payment config contradicted the Terms
**Art 5(1)(a), 12(3), 17. Severity: FOR FOUNDER AND COUNSEL TO SET.
Likelihood: MATERIALISED on both limbs.**

Two findings recorded together because they share one shape: a state of the
system that no document described, and that nothing would have surfaced.

#### (a) A full-account erasure currently deletes nothing, for anyone

Not a future risk. The purge orchestrator runs and removes zero rows.

`resolve_targets` is all-or-nothing by design — *"Preflight is all-or-nothing.
Unknown inventory means zero deletion."*, `services/data_purge.py:808`. A dependency that cannot resolve a
retention rule aborts the run rather than part-deleting an account, which is
right: a half-deleted account is worse than an undeleted one, because the
subject has been told it is finished.

Four of the five `retention_category` values are seeded by
`migrations/the_retention_schedule_is_loaded.sql`. The fifth,
`financial_evidence`, is deliberately unseeded pending document 06 §3 — so its
two dependencies land on `RETENTION_RULE_UNRESOLVED` and every purge aborts.

**If an Art 17 request arrives now it must be escalated to manual handling
inside the Art 12(3) month.** Seeding the four rules is necessary and not
sufficient; the purge begins working when counsel answers §3.

#### (b) Payment was live in production while the Terms said it was not

`STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` was present on the backend service, so checkout was
reachable — four surfaces gate on it (credits, tier, arc, webhook), each
returning `503 DISABLED` without it. The Terms draft said *"There is no paid
plan and we do not take payment."* **Zero charges have ever been taken**,
verified in the Stripe dashboard 2026-09-17, so no subject was affected; the
defect is the published statement, not the processing.

**This is the third config-versus-copy contradiction in two days** — after
OpenAI data-sharing (RISK-10) and the terms-version constant. All three were a
setting quietly disagreeing with a published document, and all three were found
by a person looking. That pattern is the finding.

**Measures:**
- **M12.1** Seed the four retention rules once document 06 is signed. **OPEN.**
- **M12.2** Counsel to answer document 06 §3 so `financial_evidence` resolves
  and erasure becomes functional. **OPEN — this is the blocker, not M12.1.**
- **M12.3** Until then, erasure requests are handled manually within Art 12(3).
  Recorded in the Art 30 record at §4 so it is not discovered mid-request.
- **M12.4** Terms and Privacy copy corrected to language that is true today and
  through the transition to paid, so a future launch needs a Terms amendment
  rather than a forced re-acceptance by every user. **Founder-approved 2026-09-17,
  applied 2026-09-18.**
- **M12.5** `STRIPE_SECRET_KEY` removed from every service. *[[FOUNDER: date,
  once verified absent from each boot log]]*
- **M12.6** `tests/test_payment_copy_contract.py` fails when payment config is

## 9. Conclusion

The processing is **lawful in design and defective in execution**. The
architecture shows genuine data-protection thought — the AC-9 fence, the blind
coach wall, the provenance separation in L3, the honest Art 9 analysis in
Privacy §3, and the AI notice receipt path are all real controls that most
services this size do not have.

Three things must change before the service takes on users at any scale:

1. **The bundled consent must be split** (RISK-1). It is the one defect capable
   of rendering the core processing unlawful.
2. **The published copy must match the code** (RISK-2).
3. **The AI Act scope question must be answered by counsel** (RISK-3), and the
   B2C fence must be enforced in code and not only in the Terms.

Subject to those, and to the open questions in §8, residual risk is assessed as
**acceptable**. The carve-out previously recorded here — the retention position
at RISK-5, elected against this assessment's advice — no longer applies: the
founder confirmed on 2026-09-17 that the authoritative schedule is the approved
one (audio 12 months after last use, measurements bound to their source), which
supersedes it. RISK-5 is downgraded to 🟢 and what remains there is execution.
